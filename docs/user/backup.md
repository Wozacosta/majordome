# Backing up and restoring your notes

Your notes and their whole history live in one folder on your computer. **Settings > Back up my
brain** keeps copies somewhere else, and brings them back when you need them.

## Where the copies can go

- **GitHub**: a private place on your GitHub account, updated after every save.
- **Backup folder**: every night, an encrypted copy in a folder you choose, such as iCloud Drive,
  Dropbox or a USB drive. Choose the folder with **Choose folder**; iCloud Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive
  and Google Drive get a button of their own when they are on the computer.
- **Google Drive**: the same encrypted copy, sent to a "Majordome backups" folder in your Drive.

The folder and Google Drive copies are locked with a passphrase you choose. You type it twice,
and Majordome never shows it: write it down on paper. Without it, nobody can open the copies, including you.

Stopping a backup never deletes the copies that were already made.

## Restore from a backup

Use this on a new computer, or after losing your notes. You need a copy and your passphrase.

1. On the welcome screen choose **Restore from a backup**, or open **Settings > Back up my brain**
   and choose **Restore from a backup** at the bottom.
2. Choose the folder your copies are in. The newest one is used. For a copy in Google Drive,
   download the newest file from the "Majordome backups" folder first, then choose the folder you
   saved it in.
3. Type your passphrase. It is used once and not kept.
4. Choose **Restore my notes**.

Your notes come back in a new folder called Majordome in your home folder, or in another place you
pick. Nothing that is already on the computer is changed: if a Majordome folder is there, the new
one is called Majordome 2. Backups are not set up on the restored notes; turn them on again in
**Back up my brain**.

Notes backed up to GitHub are not restored from this screen yet.

The copies can also be opened without Majordome; **Without Majordome**, under the restore form,
shows how.
